# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Enso Voice serves Spanish-speaking business owners and operators in the Enso community who already run real work and want to remove the keyboard as a bottleneck. They dictate messages and documents, capture meetings, turn speech into structured notes, and expect the app to stay out of their way. A small internal Enso team uses the same product with a larger managed-cloud allowance.

## Product Purpose

Enso Voice turns speech into useful text, notes, and meeting records from a desktop hotkey. It combines private on-device processing, bring-your-own-key providers, and a controlled Enso cloud allowance. Success means a member can install the app, authenticate, and complete a reliable first dictation in under three minutes while always understanding where audio is processed and how many managed minutes remain.

## Brand Personality

Serene, precise, and human. The interface should feel as fast and dependable as Raycast without becoming cold or technical for its own sake. Enso's human judgment remains visible: the product assists the user's intent instead of pretending to replace it.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI software with purple gradients, neon glows, glass panels, or chatbot-first framing.
- Dense administrative dashboards that make dictation feel like enterprise configuration.
- Gamified credit wallets, artificial urgency, and upgrade pressure inside a community benefit.
- Decorative motion, unfamiliar controls, or styling that competes with the current task.
- Any departure from the established Enso identity without an explicit product reason.

## Design Principles

1. Voice first: the shortest path from hotkey to pasted text wins.
2. Processing is legible: local, BYOK, and Enso Cloud are always named clearly.
3. Credits inform rather than pressure; depletion always offers a safe fallback.
4. Premium means consistency, speed, recovery, and excellent states—not decoration.
5. Local data remains useful when cloud access, membership, or the network disappears.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Every workflow must support keyboard navigation, visible focus, screen-reader labels, non-color state cues, and a complete reduced-motion mode. Spanish and English copy must fit without truncation. Microphone, accessibility, and recording permissions must explain why they are needed and how to recover when denied.
